Vayu Motors and the Swap Station Gamble

One founder, ten weeks, and four ways to find ₹15 crore. Here is the whole case and our answer, in a few minutes.

⚡ Our pick: Option A, the NordGreen round

45 of 150 stations are live. Each block is 10 stations. The rest has to be built in 10 weeks.

The story in short

Meera Iyer runs Vayu Swap, a battery swap unit inside scooter maker Vayu Motors. Jitan Foods, a huge delivery fleet, will give a three year exclusive deal to whoever has 150 stations live before the pre-monsoon rush. Meera has 45. A richer rival is also in the race.

Four roads, one map

Every option buys something and costs something. This chart shows the trade at a glance: how fast you get there, and how clean the route is.

Our pick
A

NordGreen round

₹15 Cr for 12% of Vayu Swap. In return, a 90 day technical and ESG audit.

Cleanest. Likely misses the deadline.

B

Chawla Capital

₹15 Cr in a week, as debt plus a revenue share. The station lease must go through his firm, 18% above market.

Fast. Hidden strings.

C

Rohan's shortcut

Sell used battery cells as new for about 60% of stations. Cuts cost by around 40%.

Real fire risk. Echoes Arjun's 2017 shortcut.

D

Subsidy sprint

Build only 30 safe stations, then push swap counts so the network looks 3 times bigger.

Legal on paper. Misleading in practice.

Why we picked Option A

It is the only road with an outsider checking the work, and the only one nobody has to hide later. We know it likely costs us the first deadline, and we plan for that.

What it costs

  • Almost certainly misses the 10 week window.
  • Meera must renegotiate with Jitan Foods. No guarantee they agree.
  • 90 days of audit prep and scrutiny.
  • A rival could sign Jitan in the meantime.

What it buys

  • A clean cap table with no side deals.
  • A respected climate fund that opens doors for Series A.
  • Divya's sign-off, the only option that earns it.
  • A governance record that survives any future audit.

Why it holds up

We tested the decision with six lenses. Tap each one for the plain English version.

🏢IntrapreneurshipPinchot and Burgelman

Meera runs a startup inside a bigger company. That only works if the parent really lets go. Today, any decision above ₹2 Cr needs Arjun's personal yes.

With NordGreen on board, big calls need board disclosure, not one person's nod. It is the same lesson as Jony Ive at Apple from class: give people real resources and real independence, and they build big things.

🎭Agency theoryJensen and Meckling

Rohan wants his sales numbers. Yogesh wants rent for his own firm. Arjun once kept secrets to survive. Each gains from their choice while someone else carries the risk.

Under B, C and D, we rely on trust. Under A, an outside audit replaces trust with process.

📡Signalling theoryCapital stack

Who funds you tells the next investor who you are. A respected climate fund says "audited and serious". A hidden related-party lease says "this team hides things", and investors find those quickly.

🌍Stakeholder theory and triple bottom lineFreeman

People: riders stay safe and staff are not asked to cut corners. Planet: only full-cycle tested batteries enter circulation. Profit: slower now, but it keeps the Series A door open.

The stakeholder we hurt most is Jitan Foods, through timing. Not through safety.

🏜️Valley of DeathInnovation cycle

Vayu Swap has 45 stations and covers its costs, but cannot fund its own growth. That is the fragile middle stage. Hidden liabilities hurt most here, so patient and clean capital fits best.

⚖️Ethics: duty over outcomeDeontological lens

Honest labels and full disclosure are duties. They do not switch off because a deadline is close. We use this lens plainly, and we also show the business case on its own, so the choice does not rest on principle alone.

The thing Meera cannot see

She admires Arjun. But he has two old secrets, and she knows neither. Tap to see the gap.

What Meera knows

  • Arjun gave her a real budget, a P&L and eighteen months.
  • He likes speed over paperwork.
  • Rohan's plan sells refurbished cells as new.
  • She has one week to decide.

What she does not know

  • 2017: cells were relabelled and certification fast-tracked without full tests.
  • 2019: a loan against family property was kept from the co-founder and board.
  • Rohan's plan copies the 2017 move almost exactly.
  • The culture she works in has a history of trading integrity for survival.

What signal do we send, and how do we manage it for two years?

We tell investors, staff and Jitan Foods the same thing: we pick credibility over speed.

Month 1Meera meets Jitan's procurement lead in person with the term sheet and a phased plan.
Months 1 to 12Publish quarterly rollout updates and rider safety data.
Month 3Share the clean audit result with fleet buyers and the board.
Months 12 to 24Use the track record as the base of the Series A data room.

If Arjun's 2017 shortcut goes public the month we launch?

Meera joined in 2020, so she had no part in it and no knowledge of it. The audit gives her a dated before and after: what she inherited and what she built.

She cooperates with an independent review of 2017, shares the clean audit with Jitan and the board, and does not defend or own what she did not do. Vayu Motors still takes a reputation hit. Her credibility holds.

The biggest risk we accept, and how we spot it in two quarters

Lens: Valley of Death. Right capital at the wrong time can still strand a company. The risk is Jitan signing the rival before our audit ends.

Signal 1Jitan announces a rival exclusive, any time before week 12.
Signal 2Bengaluru station use falls under 60% by the end of quarter 2.
Signal 3NordGreen adds audit conditions that push past day 75.

If any signal fires: emergency board review, and consider a bridge loan that is fully disclosed to the board and NordGreen.
